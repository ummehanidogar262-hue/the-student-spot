📚 Weather & Climate Guide

Weather describes the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time. Climate describes the usual pattern of weather in a place over a long period.

🌦️ Weather:
The condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time, including temperature, wind, clouds, humidity and precipitation.
🌍 Climate:
The long-term pattern of weather experienced by a place or region.

🌡️ 1. Temperature

Definition: Temperature is a measure of how hot or cold the air or another substance is. It is commonly measured in degrees Celsius (°C).

Temperature is one of the most important elements of weather. Thermometers are used to measure temperature.

💨 2. Wind

Definition: Wind is moving air. Air moves from areas of higher air pressure toward areas of lower air pressure.

Wind speed can be measured with an anemometer, while wind direction can be shown using a wind vane.

💧 3. Humidity

Definition: Humidity is the amount of water vapour present in the air. Relative humidity is commonly expressed as a percentage.

When humidity is high, the air contains a large amount of water vapour. Humidity affects cloud formation, rainfall and how comfortable the air feels.

🌧️ 4. Rainfall & Precipitation

Definition: Precipitation is water that falls from clouds to Earth's surface. It can occur as rain, snow, sleet or hail.

Rainfall is one form of precipitation. Other forms include snow, sleet and hail.

☁️ 5. Clouds

Definition: A cloud is a visible collection of tiny water droplets or ice crystals suspended in the atmosphere.

Clouds form when moist air rises, cools and water vapour condenses into tiny droplets or ice crystals.

🌍 6. Climate Zones

Definition: Climate zones are broad regions of Earth that have similar long-term patterns of temperature and precipitation.

The major climate zones include tropical, temperate and polar regions. Climate is influenced by latitude, altitude, distance from the sea and other factors.

🌪️ 7. Weather Events

Definition: Weather events are atmospheric events that can cause noticeable changes in weather conditions, such as thunderstorms, cyclones, heat waves, heavy rainfall and hailstorms.

Severe weather events can affect people, buildings, transport, agriculture and the environment. Weather warnings help people prepare for dangerous conditions.

🧠 Weather & Climate — 55 Short Questions with Answers

1. What is weather?
Answer: Weather is the condition of the atmosphere at a particular place and time.
2. What is climate?
Answer: Climate is the usual pattern of weather in a place over a long period.
3. What is temperature?
Answer: Temperature is a measure of how hot or cold something is.
4. Which instrument measures temperature?
Answer: A thermometer measures temperature.
5. What unit is commonly used to measure temperature?
Answer: Degrees Celsius, written as °C, is commonly used.
6. Why does temperature vary during the day?
Answer: Temperature changes because the amount of solar heating changes during the day.
7. What is solar energy?
Answer: Solar energy is energy received from the Sun.
8. What is wind?
Answer: Wind is moving air.
9. What causes wind?
Answer: Differences in air pressure cause air to move and produce wind.
10. In which direction does air generally move between pressure areas?
Answer: Air generally moves from higher pressure toward lower pressure.
11. What is wind speed?
Answer: Wind speed is the rate at which air moves.
12. Which instrument measures wind speed?
Answer: An anemometer measures wind speed.
13. What is wind direction?
Answer: Wind direction tells us where the wind is coming from.
14. Which instrument shows wind direction?
Answer: A wind vane shows wind direction.
15. What is air pressure?
Answer: Air pressure is the force exerted by the weight of the atmosphere.
16. What is humidity?
Answer: Humidity is the amount of water vapour present in the air.
17. What is water vapour?
Answer: Water vapour is water in its gaseous state in the atmosphere.
18. What is relative humidity?
Answer: Relative humidity compares the amount of water vapour in the air with the maximum amount the air can hold at that temperature.
19. How is relative humidity expressed?
Answer: Relative humidity is usually expressed as a percentage.
20. What happens when moist air cools?
Answer: Water vapour may condense into tiny water droplets or ice crystals.
21. What is precipitation?
Answer: Precipitation is water that falls from clouds to Earth's surface.
22. Name four forms of precipitation.
Answer: Rain, snow, sleet and hail are forms of precipitation.
23. What is rainfall?
Answer: Rainfall is liquid water that falls from clouds to Earth's surface.
24. What is snow?
Answer: Snow is precipitation that falls as ice crystals or groups of ice crystals.
25. What is hail?
Answer: Hail consists of balls or pieces of ice that fall from certain storm clouds.
26. What is condensation?
Answer: Condensation is the change of water vapour into liquid water.
27. What is evaporation?
Answer: Evaporation is the change of liquid water into water vapour.
28. What is the water cycle?
Answer: The water cycle is the continuous movement of water between Earth's surface, atmosphere and other parts of the environment.
29. What is a cloud?
Answer: A cloud is a visible collection of tiny water droplets or ice crystals in the atmosphere.
30. How do clouds form?
Answer: Clouds form when moist air rises, cools and water vapour condenses.
31. Why are clouds important?
Answer: Clouds are important because they influence weather and are a major source of precipitation.
32. What is a cloud made of?
Answer: Clouds are made of tiny water droplets, ice crystals, or both.
33. What is a climate zone?
Answer: A climate zone is a broad region with a similar long-term climate.
34. What are the three major climate zones?
Answer: The three major zones are tropical, temperate and polar.
35. What is the tropical zone?
Answer: The tropical zone is the region around the Equator that generally has warm temperatures throughout the year.
36. What is the temperate zone?
Answer: Temperate zones generally have moderate temperatures and greater seasonal changes than tropical regions.
37. What are polar zones?
Answer: Polar zones are the cold regions near the North and South Poles.
38. What is the Equator?
Answer: The Equator is an imaginary line around Earth at 0° latitude.
39. Why are tropical regions generally warm?
Answer: Tropical regions receive relatively direct solar energy throughout the year.
40. What is latitude?
Answer: Latitude is the angular distance of a place north or south of the Equator.
41. How does latitude affect climate?
Answer: Latitude affects the angle and amount of solar energy received, influencing temperature and climate.
42. How does altitude affect temperature?
Answer: Temperature generally decreases as altitude increases in the lower atmosphere.
43. How can distance from the sea affect climate?
Answer: Large bodies of water heat and cool more slowly than land, which can moderate nearby temperatures.
44. What is a weather event?
Answer: A weather event is an atmospheric event that produces noticeable weather conditions.
45. What is a thunderstorm?
Answer: A thunderstorm is a storm associated with lightning and thunder, often accompanied by heavy rain and strong winds.
46. What is lightning?
Answer: Lightning is a powerful electrical discharge in the atmosphere.
47. What is thunder?
Answer: Thunder is the sound produced by the rapid expansion of air heated by lightning.
48. What is a cyclone?
Answer: A cyclone is a large rotating weather system associated with low atmospheric pressure.
49. What is a heat wave?
Answer: A heat wave is a period of unusually hot weather lasting for several days or longer in a particular region.
50. What is a drought?
Answer: A drought is a prolonged period of unusually low precipitation that can result in water shortages.
51. What is a flood?
Answer: A flood occurs when water covers land that is normally dry.
52. What is a weather forecast?
Answer: A weather forecast is a prediction of future atmospheric conditions.
53. Why are weather forecasts useful?
Answer: Weather forecasts help people plan activities and prepare for potentially dangerous weather.
54. What is the difference between weather and climate?
Answer: Weather describes short-term atmospheric conditions, while climate describes long-term patterns of weather.
55. Why is it important to study weather and climate?
Answer: Studying weather and climate helps us understand atmospheric conditions, prepare for hazards, manage resources and understand Earth's environment.
