✍️ C1 English Writing

Learn how to write clearly, accurately and effectively.

1. Planning Your Writing

Strong writing begins before you write the first sentence. Understand the question, identify your purpose and organise your ideas before you start.

Simple planning method:
  1. Understand the question.
  2. Identify the audience.
  3. Decide your purpose.
  4. Choose your main ideas.
  5. Arrange your ideas logically.

2. Writing a Strong Introduction

An introduction should make the topic clear and prepare the reader for the main argument or ideas.

Example:

In recent years, the role of technology in education has become increasingly significant. While digital tools can provide students with greater access to information, their effectiveness depends largely on how they are used.
💡 Avoid starting every essay with: "Nowadays, technology is very important." Try to make your opening more specific and purposeful.

3. Paragraph Structure

A strong academic paragraph often contains:

Point → Explanation → Evidence/Example → Link
Example:

One major advantage of online learning is its flexibility. Students can often access lessons at times that suit their individual schedules. For example, recorded lectures allow learners to review difficult concepts whenever necessary. Consequently, online education can be particularly useful for students with demanding schedules.

4. Linking Words

Linking expressions help your writing flow logically.

Adding ideas:
furthermore, moreover, in addition, additionally

Contrasting:
however, nevertheless, whereas, although

Giving examples:
for instance, for example, such as

Showing results:
therefore, consequently, as a result

Concluding:
overall, in conclusion, to sum up

5. Formal Emails

Formal emails require polite and professional language.

Useful phrases:

Dear Sir or Madam,
I am writing to enquire about...
I would be grateful if you could...
I look forward to hearing from you.
Yours faithfully,
❌ Hey! I want some information about your course.

✅ Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to enquire about your course.

6. Writing Reports

Reports are usually organised with clear headings and objective language.

Useful structure:
  1. Introduction
  2. Findings
  3. Discussion
  4. Recommendations
  5. Conclusion
Useful phrase:
The findings indicate that the majority of students would benefit from additional study resources.

7. Opinion Essays

When writing an opinion essay, make your position clear and support it with logical reasons.

Useful phrases:

From my perspective...
It could be argued that...
One significant advantage is...
A major drawback is...
This suggests that...

8. Advanced Vocabulary in Writing

Advanced writing does not mean using complicated words everywhere. Choose precise words that communicate your meaning accurately.

Instead of: very important
Try: crucial / significant / fundamental

Instead of: make better
Try: enhance / improve / strengthen

Instead of: make worse
Try: exacerbate / aggravate

9. Avoiding Repetition

Repeating the same word too often can make writing less effective. Use precise alternatives when appropriate.

Weak:
Technology is important. Technology helps students. Technology also provides information.

Better:
Technology plays a significant role in education. Digital tools provide students with greater access to information and can support independent learning.

10. Editing and Proofreading

Never assume that your first draft is your final draft. Always review your work.

Check for:
  • Grammar
  • Spelling
  • Punctuation
  • Paragraph structure
  • Repetition
  • Clarity
  • Word choice
💡 Read your writing slowly. You may notice mistakes that you missed when reading quickly.

    B) essential
    C) optional
    D) uncertain
