📚 English Grammar

Learn English grammar step by step from A1 beginner to C2 proficiency.

🌟 Your English Grammar Journey

English grammar becomes more advanced as your language skills grow. The Student Spot organizes grammar into six CEFR levels: A1, A2, B1, B2, C1 and C2.

Start with the level that matches your current English ability and gradually move toward more advanced grammar.

A1 A2 B1 B2 C1 C2

🟢 A1 — Beginner

A1 grammar focuses on the basic structures needed to communicate about yourself, everyday activities, people, places and simple situations.

1. Subject Pronouns

Subject pronouns tell us who performs an action.

Examples:
I am a student.
She is my friend.
They live here.

2. Verb "Be" — am, is, are

Use am with I, is with he, she and it, and are with you, we and they.

I am happy.
He is twelve.
We are students.

3. Articles — a, an, the

Articles are used before nouns. Use a before a consonant sound, an before a vowel sound, and the when referring to something specific.

a book — an apple — the teacher

4. Plural Nouns

Many nouns form their plural by adding -s or -es.

book → books
class → classes

5. Possessive Adjectives

Possessive adjectives show who something belongs to.

my book — your bag — his pen — her phone — our school — their house

6. Present Simple

The present simple is commonly used for habits, routines, facts and repeated actions.

I study every day.
She plays tennis on Saturday.

7. Basic Questions

Common question words include who, what, where, when, why and how.

Where do you live?
What is your name?
How are you?

8. Can and Can't

Can expresses ability or possibility. Can't expresses inability.

I can swim.
He can't drive.

9. Basic Prepositions

Common prepositions include in, on, at, under, behind and next to.

The book is on the table.
The bag is under the chair.

10. There is / There are

Use these structures to say that something exists.

There is a book on the desk.
There are three students in the room.
💡 A1 Tip: Focus on making short, correct sentences before trying complicated structures.

🔵 A2 — Elementary

A2 grammar builds on basic English and helps you describe past events, future plans, comparisons and everyday situations in greater detail.

1. Past Simple

Use the past simple for completed actions in the past.

I visited my grandmother yesterday.
She watched a movie last night.

2. Past Simple Questions and Negatives

Use did for many past simple questions and negatives.

Did you study?
I did not study.

3. Present Continuous

Use the present continuous for actions happening now or around the present.

I am reading a book.
They are playing football.

4. Future with Going To

Use going to for plans and intentions.

I am going to study tonight.
We are going to visit the museum.

5. Will for the Future

Will can be used for predictions, decisions and promises.

I think it will rain.
I will help you.

6. Comparatives

Comparatives compare two people or things.

This book is cheaper than that one.
Ali is taller than Ahmed.

7. Superlatives

Superlatives compare one thing with a larger group.

This is the tallest building in the city.

8. Countable and Uncountable Nouns

Countable nouns can be counted. Uncountable nouns are generally treated as substances or concepts rather than individual units.

three books — some water

9. Some and Any

These words are commonly used with quantities of things.

I have some apples.
Do you have any water?

10. Basic Modal Verbs

Modal verbs such as should, must and might express advice, necessity or possibility.

You should study.
You must wear a seat belt.
💡 A2 Tip: Practise describing what you did, what you are doing and what you plan to do.

🟡 B1 — Intermediate

B1 grammar helps learners connect ideas, discuss experiences, explain reasons and use several important verb structures.

1. Present Perfect

The present perfect connects past actions or experiences with the present.

I have finished my homework.
She has visited London.

2. Present Perfect with For and Since

For describes a period of time. Since identifies when a period began.

I have lived here for three years.
She has studied English since 2022.

3. Past Continuous

Use the past continuous for an action that was in progress at a particular time in the past.

I was studying at eight o'clock.

4. Past Perfect

The past perfect describes an earlier past action in relation to another past event.

The train had left before we arrived.

5. First Conditional

The first conditional describes a possible future situation and its result.

If it rains, we will stay home.

6. Second Conditional

The second conditional is commonly used for hypothetical or unlikely situations.

If I had more time, I would learn another language.

7. Passive Voice

The passive focuses on the action or its receiver rather than the person who performs the action.

The window was broken.

8. Reported Speech

Reported speech communicates what someone said without quoting their exact words.

Direct: Sara said, "I am tired."
Reported: Sara said that she was tired.

9. Relative Clauses

Relative clauses provide additional information about a noun.

The student who won the prize is my friend.

10. Gerunds and Infinitives

Some verbs are followed by an -ing form, while others can be followed by an infinitive.

I enjoy reading.
I want to learn.
💡 B1 Tip: Practise combining simple sentences with because, although, if, when and relative clauses.

🟠 B2 — Upper-Intermediate

B2 grammar allows learners to express complex ideas, uncertainty, hypothetical situations and relationships between events more precisely.

1. Third Conditional

The third conditional describes an unreal past situation and its imagined past result.

If I had studied harder, I would have passed the exam.

2. Mixed Conditionals

Mixed conditionals connect different time periods in a hypothetical situation.

If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now.

3. Modal Verbs of Deduction

Modal verbs such as must, might and can't can express different levels of certainty.

She must be tired.
He might be at home.
They can't be serious.

4. Advanced Passive Structures

Passive structures can be combined with reporting verbs and other complex constructions.

The project is believed to be successful.

5. Advanced Reported Speech

Reported speech can include reporting verbs such as advise, warn, admit, deny and suggest.

The teacher advised us to revise carefully.

6. Participle Clauses

Participle clauses can make sentences more concise by combining related information.

Walking home, I saw an old friend.

7. Advanced Relative Clauses

Relative structures can add detailed information to complex sentences.

The scientist whose research changed medicine received an award.

8. Wish and If Only

These structures can express wishes, regrets or dissatisfaction.

I wish I had more time.
If only I had listened.

9. Causative Structures

Causative structures describe arranging for someone else to do something.

I had my laptop repaired.

10. Linking and Complex Clauses

B2 learners should be able to connect ideas using a wide range of subordinating and linking structures.

Although the exam was difficult, she completed it successfully.
💡 B2 Tip: Don't only learn grammar rules. Practise choosing the structure that communicates your intended meaning most naturally.

🔴 C1 — Advanced

C1 grammar focuses on sophisticated structures, precision, emphasis, formal writing and subtle differences in meaning.

1. Inversion

Inversion changes the normal word order, often for emphasis or formal effect.

Never have I seen such a beautiful view.

2. Cleft Sentences

Cleft structures highlight a particular part of a sentence.

It was Sara who solved the problem.

3. Advanced Conditionals

C1 learners should be able to use complex hypothetical and conditional structures accurately.

Had I known about the problem, I would have acted differently.

4. Advanced Modal Structures

Modals can express subtle degrees of certainty, criticism, expectation and possibility.

You should have told me earlier.
She may have misunderstood the instructions.

5. Subjunctive Structures

The mandative subjunctive is used in some formal expressions, particularly in formal English.

It is essential that every student be prepared.

6. Nominalisation

Nominalisation changes verbs or adjectives into noun forms and is common in formal and academic writing.

The committee decided to change the policy.
The committee made a decision to change the policy.

7. Ellipsis

Ellipsis removes words that can be understood from context.

I can play the guitar, and my sister can too.

8. Advanced Linking Structures

C1 learners should use precise structures to show contrast, cause, consequence and qualification.

Much as I understand your point, I disagree with the conclusion.

9. Reduced Relative Clauses

Some relative clauses can be reduced using participle structures.

Students studying abroad often experience new cultures.

10. Formal Grammar and Register

C1 learners need to choose grammatical structures appropriate for formal, neutral and informal situations.

Formal: I would appreciate your response.
Informal: I'd really appreciate a reply.
💡 C1 Tip: At advanced level, grammar is not only about correctness. Pay attention to emphasis, tone, precision and context.

🟣 C2 — Proficiency

C2 grammar represents highly advanced control of English. The focus is on precision, flexibility, nuance, sophisticated sentence structures and appropriate grammatical choices.

1. Sophisticated Inversion

C2 users can use inversion naturally for emphasis, literary effect and formal communication.

Little did they realise how important the decision would become.

2. Advanced Emphasis

C2 grammar allows speakers and writers to emphasize information through several structural choices.

What matters most is how the problem is solved.

3. Nuanced Modality

Advanced modal structures can communicate subtle differences between certainty, probability, obligation and possibility.

He could have been expected to respond sooner.

4. Complex Hypothetical Structures

C2 users can combine complex hypothetical meanings while maintaining accurate relationships between time and condition.

Had circumstances been different, the outcome might well have changed.

5. Advanced Ellipsis

Ellipsis can make sophisticated writing concise while leaving the intended meaning clear from context.

Some preferred the first proposal; others, the second.

6. Complex Subordination

C2 speakers can organize several relationships between ideas using carefully controlled subordinate structures.

However carefully the evidence is examined, the conclusion remains uncertain.

7. Advanced Cleft and Pseudo-Cleft Structures

These structures can place strong emphasis on particular information.

What surprised me most was the speed of the response.

8. Grammatical Choices and Nuance

At C2, different grammatically correct structures may communicate slightly different meanings, attitudes or degrees of certainty.

You must have forgotten.
You may have forgotten.

9. Formal and Literary Structures

C2 learners can understand and use sophisticated structures found in academic, professional and literary English.

Were the proposal to be accepted, significant changes would follow.

10. Flexible Sentence Construction

C2 users can restructure sentences to achieve clarity, emphasis, rhythm, formality or a particular rhetorical effect.

Only after reviewing the evidence did the researchers change their conclusion.
💡 C2 Tip: C2 is about control and flexibility. Learn to choose between different grammatical structures according to meaning, context, register and emphasis.

🎯 How to Use This Page

If you are new to English grammar, begin with A1 and work upward. If you already understand basic grammar, choose the level that matches your current ability.

Remember: CEFR levels describe overall language proficiency, so learners may have different strengths across grammar, vocabulary, reading, writing, listening and speaking.
