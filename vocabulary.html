🔤 C1 English Vocabulary

Build a sophisticated, precise and powerful vocabulary.

📚 How to use this resource

C1 vocabulary is not simply about memorising difficult words. You should understand how words behave in different contexts, what they imply, which words commonly appear with them, and how their meaning differs from similar words.

Read the meaning, study the example, learn the synonym, and then test yourself with the practice section.

1. Alleviate

Meaning: To make something unpleasant, painful, or difficult less severe.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: The new policy was introduced to alleviate financial pressure on low-income families.
Synonyms: ease, relieve, lessen

2. Ambiguous

Meaning: Having more than one possible interpretation and therefore being unclear.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The instructions were ambiguous, so several students interpreted them differently.
Synonyms: unclear, equivocal, vague

3. Apprehensive

Meaning: Worried or nervous about something that may happen.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: She felt apprehensive about speaking in front of such a large audience.
Synonyms: anxious, uneasy, concerned

4. Articulate

Meaning: Able to express ideas clearly, fluently and effectively.

Part of speech: Adjective / Verb

Example: He was able to articulate his argument clearly during the debate.
Synonyms: eloquent, expressive, well-spoken

5. Assertive

Meaning: Confidently expressing opinions, needs or rights without being unnecessarily aggressive.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: An assertive student can disagree respectfully while clearly explaining their position.
Synonyms: confident, self-assured, decisive

6. Coherent

Meaning: Logical, clear and well connected.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The essay presented a coherent argument supported by evidence.
Synonyms: logical, consistent, organised

7. Compelling

Meaning: Extremely convincing, persuasive, or interesting.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The researcher provided compelling evidence to support the theory.
Synonyms: convincing, persuasive, powerful

8. Conspicuous

Meaning: Very noticeable or easy to see.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Her absence was particularly conspicuous during the final presentation.
Synonyms: noticeable, prominent, obvious

9. Contemplate

Meaning: To think about something carefully for a period of time.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: Before making the decision, she contemplated all the possible consequences.
Synonyms: consider, reflect on, ponder

10. Contradict

Meaning: To state the opposite of what someone else has said or to conflict with something.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: The witness's second statement appeared to contradict her earlier account.
Synonyms: oppose, conflict with, dispute

11. Conventional

Meaning: Based on traditional or generally accepted ideas, methods or behaviour.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The company rejected conventional approaches and adopted a more innovative strategy.
Synonyms: traditional, standard, customary

12. Credible

Meaning: Able to be believed or trusted.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The journalist relied on several credible sources.
Synonyms: believable, trustworthy, convincing

13. Cumulative

Meaning: Increasing as a result of successive additions.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The cumulative effect of several small changes was substantial.
Synonyms: accumulated, increasing, collective

14. Deteriorate

Meaning: To become worse in quality, condition or effectiveness.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: Without regular maintenance, the condition of the building will deteriorate.
Synonyms: worsen, decline, degrade

15. Diligent

Meaning: Showing careful and persistent effort.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Her diligent preparation helped her perform confidently in the examination.
Synonyms: hardworking, conscientious, industrious

16. Diminish

Meaning: To become or make something smaller, weaker or less important.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: The discovery did not diminish the importance of the earlier research.
Synonyms: reduce, lessen, weaken

17. Discrepancy

Meaning: A difference between two things that should be similar or consistent.

Part of speech: Noun

Example: The accountant noticed a discrepancy between the two financial reports.
Synonyms: inconsistency, difference, divergence

18. Elaborate

Meaning: Containing many details or developed carefully.

Part of speech: Adjective / Verb

Example: The scientist gave an elaborate explanation of the process.
Synonyms: detailed, complex, expand upon

19. Empirical

Meaning: Based on observation, experience or evidence rather than theory alone.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The researchers collected empirical evidence before drawing conclusions.
Synonyms: evidence-based, observed, experimental

20. Enhance

Meaning: To improve the quality, value or effectiveness of something.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: Regular reading can enhance both vocabulary and comprehension.
Synonyms: improve, strengthen, increase

21. Evident

Meaning: Clear or obvious from the available evidence.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: It became evident that the original plan was no longer practical.
Synonyms: obvious, apparent, clear

22. Exacerbate

Meaning: To make a problem or difficult situation worse.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: Poor communication can exacerbate an already difficult situation.
Synonyms: worsen, aggravate, intensify

23. Explicit

Meaning: Clearly stated, leaving little room for doubt.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The teacher gave explicit instructions before the examination.
Synonyms: clear, direct, unambiguous

24. Feasible

Meaning: Possible and practical to achieve.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: We need to determine whether the proposed project is financially feasible.
Synonyms: practical, achievable, viable

25. Fluctuate

Meaning: To change continually, especially between different levels or conditions.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: Energy prices tend to fluctuate throughout the year.
Synonyms: vary, oscillate, shift

26. Fundamental

Meaning: Basic and essential to a particular subject, system or idea.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Critical thinking is fundamental to effective academic study.
Synonyms: essential, basic, foundational

27. Hypothetical

Meaning: Based on an imagined situation rather than something known to be real.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The teacher presented a hypothetical situation for the students to analyse.
Synonyms: imagined, theoretical, supposed

28. Inevitable

Meaning: Certain to happen and impossible to avoid.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Some degree of change is inevitable as technology develops.
Synonyms: unavoidable, certain, inescapable

29. Infer

Meaning: To reach a conclusion based on evidence or information rather than direct statements.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: From the evidence, we can infer that the experiment was successful.
Synonyms: deduce, conclude, derive

30. Innovative

Meaning: Introducing new ideas, methods or approaches.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The school introduced an innovative approach to language learning.
Synonyms: inventive, original, pioneering

31. Integral

Meaning: Essential to making something complete or functioning properly.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Feedback is an integral part of effective learning.
Synonyms: essential, fundamental, vital

32. Intricate

Meaning: Very complicated and containing many connected or detailed parts.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The machine contains an intricate network of components.
Synonyms: complex, elaborate, complicated

33. Justify

Meaning: To provide reasons or evidence showing that something is reasonable or correct.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: Students should be able to justify their answers using evidence.
Synonyms: defend, support, substantiate

34. Meticulous

Meaning: Extremely careful and precise about details.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The scientist kept meticulous records throughout the experiment.
Synonyms: thorough, precise, painstaking

35. Notion

Meaning: An idea, belief or understanding of something.

Part of speech: Noun

Example: The notion that success depends entirely on talent is questionable.
Synonyms: idea, concept, belief

36. Obsolete

Meaning: No longer useful because something newer has replaced it.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Many older technologies have become obsolete.
Synonyms: outdated, antiquated, superseded

37. Plausible

Meaning: Seeming reasonable or likely to be true.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The researcher proposed a plausible explanation for the results.
Synonyms: credible, believable, reasonable

38. Preliminary

Meaning: Occurring before the main or final stage.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The preliminary results appear promising, although further research is needed.
Synonyms: initial, introductory, preparatory

39. Predominant

Meaning: More common, important or powerful than others.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Agriculture remains the predominant source of income in the region.
Synonyms: dominant, prevailing, leading

40. Proficient

Meaning: Skilled and competent at doing something.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Students are expected to become proficient in academic writing.
Synonyms: skilled, competent, capable

41. Profound

Meaning: Very deep, serious or having a strong effect.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The discovery had a profound impact on modern science.
Synonyms: deep, significant, far-reaching

42. Reluctant

Meaning: Unwilling or hesitant to do something.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: He was initially reluctant to accept the proposal.
Synonyms: unwilling, hesitant, resistant

43. Resilient

Meaning: Able to recover or adapt after difficulties.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Resilient students learn from setbacks instead of giving up.
Synonyms: adaptable, strong, persistent

44. Scrutinise

Meaning: To examine something very carefully in order to discover details or problems.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: Scientists scrutinised the data before publishing their findings.
Synonyms: examine, inspect, analyse

45. Substantial

Meaning: Large or significant in amount, size, importance or effect.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The project required a substantial amount of research.
Synonyms: considerable, significant, sizeable

46. Subtle

Meaning: Not obvious or easy to notice; involving small but important differences.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The author uses subtle changes in tone to reveal the character's emotions.
Synonyms: understated, nuanced, delicate

47. Sustainable

Meaning: Able to continue over a long period without causing unacceptable harm or using resources excessively.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: Governments are searching for more sustainable sources of energy.
Synonyms: viable, maintainable, environmentally responsible

48. Tentative

Meaning: Not certain or definite; provisional.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: We have made a tentative decision, but further discussion is necessary.
Synonyms: provisional, uncertain, preliminary

49. Thorough

Meaning: Complete and careful, leaving no important part unexamined.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The report provides a thorough analysis of the problem.
Synonyms: comprehensive, exhaustive, detailed

50. Undermine

Meaning: To gradually weaken someone's confidence, authority, position or effectiveness.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: Constant criticism can undermine a student's confidence.
Synonyms: weaken, erode, sabotage

51. Versatile

Meaning: Able to adapt to many different uses, activities or situations.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: A versatile student can adapt to different learning environments.
Synonyms: adaptable, flexible, multifaceted

52. Viable

Meaning: Capable of working successfully or being realistically achieved.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The team proposed a viable alternative to the original plan.
Synonyms: feasible, workable, practical

53. Widespread

Meaning: Existing or happening across a large area or among many people.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The discovery received widespread attention.
Synonyms: extensive, prevalent, common

54. Adverse

Meaning: Having a harmful, negative or unfavourable effect.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The medication may cause adverse reactions in some people.
Synonyms: harmful, negative, unfavourable

55. Allocate

Meaning: To distribute something for a particular purpose.

Part of speech: Verb

Example: The school allocated additional funds to improve its library.
Synonyms: assign, distribute, designate

56. Comprehensive

Meaning: Including all or nearly all important aspects of something.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The website provides a comprehensive collection of study resources.
Synonyms: complete, extensive, thorough

57. Discerning

Meaning: Showing good judgment and the ability to recognise subtle differences.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: A discerning reader can recognise the difference between strong evidence and unsupported claims.
Synonyms: perceptive, insightful, judicious

58. Inevitably

Meaning: In a way that cannot be avoided.

Part of speech: Adverb

Example: As technology develops, traditional methods will inevitably change.
Synonyms: unavoidably, certainly, necessarily

59. Subsequent

Meaning: Happening after something else.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The initial experiment failed, but subsequent attempts were successful.
Synonyms: following, later, succeeding

60. Sophisticated

Meaning: Highly developed, complex or showing considerable understanding and experience.

Part of speech: Adjective

Example: The researchers developed a sophisticated method of analysing the data.
Synonyms: advanced, refined, complex

🧠 C1 Vocabulary Practice

  1. The government introduced measures to ______ the effects of the economic crisis.

    A) exacerbate   B) alleviate   C) undermine   D) diminish
  2. The instructions were so ______ that nobody knew exactly what they were expected to do.

    A) coherent   B) explicit   C) ambiguous   D) credible
  3. The researcher presented a ______ argument supported by several independent sources.

    A) coherent   B) obsolete   C) tentative   D) adverse
  4. The company needs to determine whether the proposed plan is financially ______.

    A) inevitable   B) viable   C) conspicuous   D) cumulative
  5. Students should ______ the evidence carefully before reaching a conclusion.

    A) scrutinise   B) allocate   C) alleviate   D) diminish
  6. The discovery had a ______ impact on the field of medicine.

    A) preliminary   B) profound   C) tentative   D) conventional
  7. The school provides a ______ programme covering all major areas of the subject.

    A) comprehensive   B) ambiguous   C) obsolete   D) reluctant
  8. The new technology may eventually make some older systems ______.

    A) articulate   B) obsolete   C) diligent   D) feasible
  9. The student gave a ______ explanation containing precise details and supporting evidence.

    A) meticulous   B) ambiguous   C) adverse   D) reluctant
  10. Lack of preparation can ______ an already difficult situation.

    A) enhance   B) exacerbate   C) allocate   D) justify

✅ Answer Key

1. B — alleviate
2. C — ambiguous
3. A — coherent
4. B — viable
5. A — scrutinise
6. B — profound
7. A — comprehensive
8. B — obsolete
9. A — meticulous
10. B — exacerbate
