🗣️ English Pronunciation

Speak more clearly, naturally and confidently.

1. Why Pronunciation Matters

Good pronunciation helps other people understand you clearly. You do not need to sound exactly like a native speaker. The goal is clear, understandable and confident communication.

💡 Tip: Focus on clarity rather than trying to copy a particular accent.

2. Word Stress

Word stress means giving one syllable more emphasis than the others. The stressed syllable is usually clearer and slightly stronger.

Examples:

PHOtograph
phoTOGraphy
ecoNOmic
ecoNOmics

Notice that the position of stress can change when a word changes form.

3. Sentence Stress

In natural English, important words are usually stressed more strongly than grammatical words.

Content words usually receive stress:
nouns, main verbs, adjectives and adverbs.
Example:
I BOUGHT a NEW BOOK yesterday.

The exact words that receive stress can change depending on what the speaker wants to emphasise.

4. Intonation

Intonation is the way your voice rises and falls while speaking. It can communicate meaning, emotion and attitude.

Example:

"Really?" ↗️
A rising tone can show surprise or uncertainty.

"Really." ↘️
A falling tone can sound more definite.

5. /θ/ and /ð/ Sounds

These sounds can be challenging for many English learners.

/θ/ — think, three, author

/ð/ — this, that, mother

The two sounds are produced in a similar position, but /ð/ is voiced while /θ/ is unvoiced.

6. /ɪ/ and /iː/ Sounds

These two vowel sounds can change the meaning of a word.

Compare:

ship /ʃɪp/
sheep /ʃiːp/

live /lɪv/
leave /liːv/
💡 Listen carefully to the vowel length and quality when practising.

7. Silent Letters

Some English words contain letters that are not pronounced.

Examples:

knife → the "k" is silent
honest → the "h" is silent
write → the "w" is silent
island → the "s" is silent

8. Linking Sounds

In natural speech, words often connect together instead of being pronounced as completely separate units.

Example:

"pick it up"
"turn it off"
"an apple"

Practising connected speech can help your English sound smoother.

9. Minimal Pairs

Minimal pairs are words that differ by only one sound. They are useful for pronunciation practice.

ship — sheep
bit — beat
full — fool
fan — van
rice — rise

10. Pronunciation Practice Routine

Try this simple routine for 10 minutes a day:

  1. Choose five difficult words.
  2. Listen carefully to their pronunciation.
  3. Say each word slowly.
  4. Say each word at normal speaking speed.
  5. Put each word into a sentence.
  6. Repeat the sentences aloud.
🌟 Consistent short practice is usually more useful than one very long practice session.

🧠 Pronunciation Practice

  1. Which word contains a silent "k"?

    A) kind
    B) knife
    C) keep
    D) kitchen
  2. Which pair is a minimal pair?

    A) book / books
    B) ship / sheep
    C) teacher / student
    D) quickly / slowly
  3. Which word contains the /ð/ sound?

    A) think
    B) three
    C) this
    D) author
  4. What does sentence stress help communicate?

    A) Only spelling
    B) Important information and emphasis
    C) Only punctuation
    D) Word length
  5. What is intonation?

    A) The spelling of a word
    B) The rise and fall of the voice
    C) The number of letters in a word
    D) The speed of writing

✅ Answer Key

1. B — knife
2. B — ship / sheep
3. C — this
4. B — Important information and emphasis
5. B — The rise and fall of the voice
